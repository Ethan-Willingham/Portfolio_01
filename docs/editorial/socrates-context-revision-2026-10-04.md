# Socrates and Plato: context revision

The previous opening started with the conclusion of Plato's cave argument before identifying either philosopher or explaining the imagined city. This revision makes the article readable without prior knowledge of Greek philosophy.

The published title is **Socrates, Plato, and the cave**. The article is about 3,000 words, with a 14-minute reading estimate.

## What changed

- Introduced Socrates as a real Athenian philosopher, Plato as a younger associate and author, and dialogues as written conversations. Distinguished Plato's character from the historical Socrates.
- Used the borrowed-weapons example to show how a plausible rule fails under questioning before naming the Socratic method.
- Explained the trial's formal charges, religious setting, penalty decision, and the meaning of Apology. Followed the trial with the prison argument and final conversation.
- Reset the scene when entering the Republic. Explained the justice question, why Socrates constructs a city in words, and the relation between the city's order and a person's motives.
- Answered why Socrates thinks justice benefits a person, rather than leaving Glaucon's challenge unresolved.
- Told the cave's physical story before interpreting it. Made its imagined prisoners, fire, objects, shadows, forced ascent, and adjustment on return explicit.
- Defined allegory, soul, appetites, spirit, Forms, and the Good through the relevant arguments. Kept the objection to independent Forms and the objection to rule by claimed knowledge.
- Explained education's moral purpose and the proposed rulers' selection, training, practical experience, and debt to the city.
- Removed the numbered stops, floating navigation, four-dialogue framing, translation panels, and several repeated reservations. Preserved the existing article anchors and the religious-text title and reading style.
