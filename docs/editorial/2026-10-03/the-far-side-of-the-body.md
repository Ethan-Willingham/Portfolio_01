# The Far Side of the Body: editorial review

Revised file: `archive/the-far-side-of-the-body/the-far-side-of-the-body.html`.

The reader's payoff is a concrete comparison of abilities that can separate: pain and injury, facial recognition and familiarity, movement and agency, autobiographical recall and laboratory memory. All 21 main cases remain. Each now states what was observed, what explanation is proposed, and how far its evidence can reasonably travel.

## Original problems and decisions

The opening made the entire body a set of reversible dials. Its SCN9A diagram placed congenital pain insensitivity, ordinary sensation and erythromelalgia along one continuous gradient. That concealed differences between specific variants, inheritance and other causes. The replacement uses two separate rows, with gain-of-function and loss-of-function effects and inheritance labeled. It has a new accessible description and caption.

Several errors were consequential rather than cosmetic. The CRPS suicide percentages were not results from the linked 39-person study. The RBD percentage was a survival estimate, not an observed fraction followed for 12 years. The Cotard scan was not a brain-death or anesthesia comparison. The approved drug named by implication in the SCN9A story targets Nav1.8, not Nav1.7. KLS was described as impairing people between episodes rather than principally during episodes. Progeria was made into a complete model of accelerated ordinary aging. Several observations from one patient became claims about a whole disease.

The revision removes the repeated alarm, wire, dial and superpower language. The seventh section is now “Unusual abilities and their limits.” A person without pain is not presented as the good end of a scale. The famous patients and findings remain interesting without supplying a universal physiology.

The ten-percent cut was exceeded without losing a main case. Most cuts came from duplicated framing, dramatic tags, unverified records, weak side lists and a second dial-based ending. Five native optional disclosures hold the retained side examples; the sixth disclosure holds grouped references. The 12-minute estimate covers roughly 2,150 main words at about 180 words per minute, allowing for scientific terminology and the figure. Optional notes and sources are explicitly excluded.

1. **Monotone read:** replace claims that depended on drama with symptoms, experiment design or an observed contrast. “A hand with its own agenda” becomes unintended movement and altered agency. “A second skeleton defending itself” becomes extra bone and procedural risk.
2. **Delete the first paragraph:** the original burning/no-pain slogan did no work that the SCN9A explanation did not already do. The new first paragraph gives the specific contrasting effects, then immediately defines the channel and distinguishes variants. It earns its place as the diagram's introduction.
3. **Generic-sentence pass:** remove the body-runs-on-settings thesis, staged wing invitations, superpower reassurance, sweeping dignity ending and invented account of a fact-checker's job. Retain the specific ethical history of Pastrana in optional material.
4. **Cut ten percent:** preserve all 21 cases and shorten surrounding scaffolding. Retain enough detail to understand the numbers and their denominators rather than turn the cards into slogans.
5. **Source pass:** link every retained consequential number next to its claim. Replace loose strong/good/mixed/thin badges with explicit study types and local limits. Correct source identities and distinguish observation, proposed mechanism, cohort estimate and personal report.

The closing is one short paragraph about what a case can suggest and what wider application requires. The crisis box supplies a useful contact without another causal or moral conclusion.

## Access limits and remaining uncertainty

Several PMC routes returned anti-bot pages to the web tool. Original full text was then obtained through direct PMC retrieval or Europe PMC's original XML, rather than using snippets as substitutes. Some original papers were read in author-uploaded or scanned copies, explicitly linked above.

Cox's original full text remained paywalled; its abstract and displayed figures were checked, and the mutation/clinical claims were independently corroborated against full GeneReviews entries. Picard's stimulation report was checked through its full specialist review and original bibliographic record, not an inaccessible original methods section. Cohen's oxygen trial was available as a detailed original abstract; no dosage, response percentage or guarantee was retained. Cawein's full historical paper was inaccessible; only the general Kentucky family report remains, with present biochemical/treatment claims supported by the full expert recommendations. The Cotard author-hosted PDF was retrieved despite a certificate mismatch, then its PDF header, title and DOI were confirmed. These are candid source-access limits, not grounds for certainty about unobserved patients.

The guide is not a complete disease review or a prevalence table. Rare case mechanisms and imaging interpretations remain uncertain where their own literature says they are. No individualized treatment, survival or diagnostic prediction is made. Further length reductions would begin to remove either a case or the methodological context that repaired the main errors.

## Proposed archive-card copy

Rare conditions separate abilities we usually experience together. Twenty-one cases explore pain, sleep, memory and movement, with each study's limits kept beside the findings it can support.
