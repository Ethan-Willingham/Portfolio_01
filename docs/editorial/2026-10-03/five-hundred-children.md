# Five Hundred Children, No Books

## Payoff and scope

The reader can distinguish an imagined prediction about religious origins from evidence about particular intuitions, transmission and secular ceremonies. The question remains worth asking. The revised answer no longer treats analogy or shared structure as a measured outcome.

## Specific original problems

The standfirst promised a near-certain result that no study had measured. Section headings called the Americas and three unrelated cases repeated experiments. Caveats appeared after the persuasive claim, then were canceled by the next confident assertion.

The afterlife SVG drew a smooth decline across age without plotted data, collapsed different kinds of mental states, and implied that an age difference refuted cultural teaching. AA was presented as an empirical filter that retained only effective practices, with surviving 1935 eyewitnesses asserted in 2026. The published program was described as free of doctrine despite its repeated references to God, prayer and spiritual awakening.

The Burning Man account said nobody intended a memorial rite. Its own project history says the first Temple became a memorial after a builder died. East German secular ceremonies were counted as religion returning, making the thesis hard to falsify. The Gathas were reduced to a refusal to surrender, even though the primary text includes petitions for divine help. The ending repeatedly converted an expectation into a guarantee and invited the reader to supply a predetermined spiritual answer.

## Actual access, methods and populations

Used web browsing and directly retrieved text/PDFs where the web reader failed. Research excerpts remain in `/tmp/five-hundred-research`, outside the repository. Search snippets were discovery aids, not proof.

- **Kelemen 1999:** full PDF accessible. Inspected Study 1 methods, examples and results: 48 children, sixteen in each of first, second and fourth grade, plus sixteen undergraduates; realistic prehistoric animal/object pictures and alternatives supplied by the interviewer. Culture and missing physical knowledge were not controlled away.
- **Kelemen, Rottman and Seston 2013:** full PDF and supplement accessible. Inspected participant selection, random speed assignment, task items and results. Final Study 1 sample: 80 scientists, 179 undergraduates, 49 community adults; 71 excluded on response/comprehension criteria. Scientists were publishing English-speaking physical scientists under 45 at selected American universities. Speed limit 3.2 seconds. Increased errors are task-level evidence, not an individual diagnosis of theism.
- **Bering and Bjorklund 2004:** PubMed abstract inspected. Queen's University Belfast full PDF returned 403; ResearchGate's purported full text supplied only a scanned preview/abstract to the reader. No claim to have read the methods, no asserted full sample, no percentages and no empirical curve. The new paragraph says this beside the finding.
- **Watson-Jones et al. 2017:** full PMC text accessible through direct retrieval. Inspected participants, recruitment, random narrative assignment, results and discussion. N=228, 114 each in Texas and Tanna; age groups spanning 7 to 70. Tanna sample was 98% Presbyterian and 2% Baha'i, clearly not unexposed. Medical and supernatural narratives produced culturally different responses. The cross-cultural contrast qualifies the proposed universal body/mind pattern.
- **Lyons et al. 2007:** Yale PDF accessible. Inspected Experiment 1A training/test procedures and discussion: 63 children aged 3 to 5, mean 49 months. Adult demonstrations on transparent objects followed extensive training to identify irrelevant actions. This tests action learning, not religious practice.
- **Peoples et al. 2016:** full PMC text accessible. Inspected selection, trait definitions, coding, model and results. Twenty-eight societies selected from the Standard Cross-Cultural Sample plus five additional cases. Binary or categorical ethnographic coding, population relationships inferred through genetic/linguistic trees, and ancestral-state reconstruction with uncertain intermediate states. Not a random population sample, and modern societies are not frozen ancestors.
- **Senghas et al. 2004:** complete four-page PDF accessible. Thirty deaf signers, ten per cohort, all exposed by age 6, compared with ten hearing Spanish speakers. Cartoon motion retellings were recorded and coded for separation of manner and path. This specific grammatical property is not the whole language, and cohort comparisons are not randomized.
- **Kuitems et al. 2021:** full Nature article accessible through direct retrieval, including methods. Three worked pieces of wood, annual rings and the known radiocarbon event supply the 1021 date. Does not identify the start or end of every Norse visit, or religious transmission.
- **Ioannidis et al. 2020:** abstract and main findings accessible; methods remained behind subscription access. The stated genomic sample was 807 individuals across seventeen Polynesian islands and fifteen Native American groups. Retained only the broad evidence of contact. No new dating or voyage-direction claim.
- **Guiart 1956:** original 460-page French field report accessible. Inspected title/method setting, introductory material and selected historical pages, not the whole monograph. Printed pp. 151 and 152 describe the first Nicol report dated January 3, 1941 and missionary/administrative circumstances. Research took place in 1952 and 1953; reconstructed early events rely on records and testimony. Colonial commission and terminology limit the observer's perspective. Also consulted [Lindstrom 2022](https://journals.openedition.org/jso/pdf/12501) on that research context; it is secondary analysis, not an independently observed founding.
- **Pew Russia:** report and methods inspected. Three weighted, geographically stratified representative adult samples, ages 16+, with self-administered questionnaires in 1991, 1998 and 2008. Published total-sample error estimates roughly 3%, 3.3% and 4.4%. Identity change cannot be read as a randomized consequence of state policy.
- **Smith ISSP:** complete report accessible. Inspected comparison scope, tables and question wording. The God-belief result is for 2008; the report selects thirty jurisdictions present in at least two rounds. It does not cover every community or every kind of spiritual belief.
- **Institutional histories and primary texts:** AA history, Wilson's retrospective account, the actual Steps PDF, Burning Man's Temple history, and the Jugendweihe organizer's German page were opened and inspected. Avesta's Gathas section attributes the translation to C. Bartholomae via Taraporewala; Yasna 28 and 30 inspected. Romans read in NRSVUE and Quran 30:30 in Mustafa Khattab's translation. These are documentary or religious claims with identified authorship, not statistical findings.
- **Eye anatomy:** Hanke and Kelber's 2020 review accessible in full text. Inspected retinal organization and camera-eye comparison. Kept as an anatomical analogy, without eye-origin counts or a one-gene cultural argument.

## Residual limits

The island outcome remains unmeasured and its imagined conditions do not fully define a culture. Two source-access limits remain explicit: the Bering/Bjorklund methods and the Ioannidis methods. Early Tanna history is reconstructed testimony and archival evidence, not contemporaneous experimental observation. The secular-rite comparison makes no claim that a nonbeliever has a hidden religion. No empirical statement in the revision settles whether God exists.
