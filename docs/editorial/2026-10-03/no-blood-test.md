# The DSM: What a Diagnosis Can Tell You

Reviewed and revised on 2026-10-03.

## Reader payoff and reading map

The reader can distinguish a clinical diagnosis from a biological finding, understand what a reliability statistic measures, and ask more useful questions about a diagnosis: what supports it, what alternatives were considered, and what would justify revising it. The post remains critical of uncertain boundaries and weak agreement without suggesting that suffering is unreal or that laboratory-tested diseases have greater moral worth.

The main path has seven sections, twenty condition cards, three SVG figures and five images. It moves from the DSM's scope through attention and mood, psychosis, physical symptoms, personality and identity, addiction, and diagnostic confidence. Genetic and medical examples explain specific kinds of evidence. Fatal familial insomnia is now optional; the methods and bibliography remain in a separate disclosure. The cards are sketches, not complete diagnostic criteria or a self-assessment tool.

## Specific original problems

- The title, metadata, opening, first figure and repeated conclusions all asserted that nothing in the DSM can be biologically tested. The manual's own contents contradict this. The biological examples did not support a psychiatry-versus-medicine division.
- The Alzheimer test was called approved rather than cleared, without its age, symptom or stand-alone-diagnosis restrictions. Narcolepsy was presented as outside the manual despite appearing in it.
- The reliability discussion confused kappa with accuracy, described 0.28 as close to a coin toss, and treated the 2013 trials as a test of the newest edition. The chart mixed adult and child samples without labels or uncertainty intervals.
- Huntington disease was described as predictable down to an age. The figure blurred intermediate alleles, reduced penetrance and full penetrance.
- The anorexia mortality superlative exceeded the cited eating-disorder meta-analysis and ignored opioid populations. The text also implied that all assessment was conversational.
- The syphilis story compressed the 1913 bacterial demonstration and 1943 penicillin report into a few years. Captions overstated how decisively a tumor, copper ring or treatment settles a case.
- Several sketches were misleading: bipolar illness as alternating ordinary highs and lows, all eating disorders as body-image problems, somatic symptom disorder as symptoms without a physical cause, and personality labels as descriptions of entire people.
- The homosexuality account placed the membership vote in the wrong year and minimized the research behind the change. The bereavement account implied that normal sadness could automatically become depression after two weeks. RDoC was used as a verdict against the whole DSM rather than a research framework.
- The prevalence cards mixed denominators and dates. Repeated proof language, staged instructions and multiple endings made the post longer without adding evidence.
- The original fatal familial insomnia link, NBK1366, actually led to thanatophoric dysplasia. The schizophrenia heritability PubMed link also led to the wrong paper. The first was replaced; the second claim and its unnecessary citation were cut.

Types below separate classification facts, measurements, models, historical findings and editorial interpretation. The medical examples illustrate diagnostic evidence; they are not recommended workups for every reader.

| Depression, ADHD and autism lack a single diagnostic biological marker | Clinical fact | [MedlinePlus depression screening](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/depression-screening/), [APA ADHD](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/adhd/what-is-adhd) and [CDC autism diagnosis](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/diagnosis/index.html) support clinical assessment and tests for competing explanations. This does not mean no biological research or useful medical testing exists. |
| Alzheimer blood testing and ADHD EEG | Regulatory facts | [FDA's 2025 clearance](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-clears-first-blood-test-used-diagnosing-alzheimers-disease) concerns an aid for symptomatic adults aged 55 or older, not screening or stand-alone diagnosis. The [2013 NEBA review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/k112711.pdf) likewise describes an adjunct. FDA device clearance does not establish that any test independently diagnoses every presentation. |
| ADHD heritability around 74 percent | Twin-study model | [Faraone and Larsson](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-018-0070-0) review twin studies. Full penetrance assumes a normal lifespan; onset varies. The replacement categorical figure and adjacent text state the distinction. Counseling remains relevant to predictive testing. |
| Short clinical sketches | Clinical descriptions | Read APA guides for [depression](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/depression/what-is-depression), [anxiety](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/anxiety-disorders/what-are-anxiety-disorders), [bipolar disorders](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/bipolar-disorders/what-are-bipolar-disorders) and [schizophrenia](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/schizophrenia/what-is-schizophrenia). Retained only broad defining patterns, plus consequential timing and bipolar I/II distinctions. |
| Psychosis timing distinguishes several neighboring diagnoses | Clinical descriptions | Read the clinical references for [schizoaffective disorder](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK541012/), [brief psychotic disorder](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK539912/) and [delusional disorder](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/schizophrenia-and-related-disorders/delusional-disorder). Added sources to individual cards. History, exclusions and course matter; the cards still omit many criteria. |
| Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis can initially present psychiatrically | Medical example | Read [Graus et al.'s diagnostic guidance](https://escholarship.org/content/qt51r4z8nx/qt51r4z8nx.pdf?v=lg), including its antibody and CSF discussion. Diagnosis combines clinical findings, appropriate antibody testing and exclusions. Tumors occur in some cases; removal is part of treatment when applicable. Cut the memoir's unverified clock-test anecdote. |
| Neurosyphilis discovery rapidly emptied wards | Historical claim | [Noguchi and Moore's original paper](https://rupress.org/jem/article-abstract/17/2/232/6861/A-DEMONSTRATION-OF-TREPONEMA-PALLIDUM-IN-THE-BRAIN?redirectedFrom=fulltext) dates the demonstration to 1913. [Mahoney's report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1527687/) dates early-syphilis penicillin treatment to 1943. Removed unsupported ward-emptying claims. [CDC guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/neurosyphilis.htm) supports present evaluation and treatment; [CDC's syphilis page](https://www.cdc.gov/syphilis/about/index.html) notes that injury may persist. |
| Anorexia is deadlier than every other mental illness | Unsupported universal ranking | Read [Arcelus et al.'s full article](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/1107207), including methods, heterogeneity and diagnostic crossover. Its pooled standardized mortality ratio is 5.86, from eating-disorder studies with varying follow-up. [Degenhardt et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1360-0443.2010.03140.x) report higher relative mortality in regular or dependent opioid cohorts. The populations and reference rates differ; neither establishes a universal ranking or individual prognosis. |
| PTSD, OCD, eating disorders and somatic symptoms | Clinical descriptions | Read APA guides for [PTSD](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/ptsd/what-is-ptsd), [OCD](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/obsessive-compulsive-disorder/what-is-obsessive-compulsive-disorder), [eating disorders](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/eating-disorders/what-are-eating-disorders) and [somatic symptom disorder](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/somatic-symptom-disorder/what-is-somatic-symptom-disorder). Corrected insight variability, weight/body-image generalization and the medically-unexplained-symptom assumption. |
| Narcolepsy type 1 has a biological diagnostic route | Medical example and criterion | Read [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/narcolepsy) and [the clinical criteria reference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459236/?report=printable). Retained hypocretin-1 at or below 110 pg/mL or below one-third of the normal laboratory mean, with the appropriate clinical setting and sleep-testing route. It is not a test for ordinary tiredness. |
| Personality and dissociative identity diagnoses | Clinical descriptions | [APA personality disorders](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/personality-disorders/what-are-personality-disorders) supports enduring patterns, impairment and cultural context. [APA dissociative disorders](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/dissociative-disorders/what-are-dissociative-disorders) supports identity disruption and memory gaps. Separated the DSM groups and removed casual labels as clinical verdicts. |
| Fatal familial insomnia can be definitively predicted | Genetic example | Corrected the source to [Genetic Prion Disease, GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1229/). Compatible findings plus a pathogenic PRNP variant differ from an uncertain variant or a predicted onset date. Cut exact survival claims and moved this rare example into optional detail. |
| Substance use disorders, gambling and gaming status | Classification and clinical facts | Read [APA's 2013 changes](https://www.psychiatry.org/file%20library/psychiatrists/practice/dsm/apa_dsm-5-substance-use-disorder.pdf), [substance use guide](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/addiction-substance-use-disorders/what-is-a-substance-use-disorder), [gambling guide](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/gambling-disorder/what-is-gambling-disorder) and [gaming page](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/internet-gaming). Gaming remains a condition for further study in DSM-5-TR. [APA opioid guidance](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/opioid-use-disorder) distinguishes supervised physiological dependence from OUD; [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/drug-testing/) distinguishes evidence of exposure from a disorder and discusses confirmation. |
| Vietnam veterans demonstrate an environmental cure for addiction | Observational cohort, not causal experiment | Read [the complete original three-year follow-up, reprinted in 2010](https://gwern.net/doc/psychiatry/2010-robins.pdf). Retained about one in five addicted in Vietnam and 12 percent reporting return to addiction over three years, tied to the weighted 1971 Army cohort. Changed availability, circumstances and treatment do not isolate a single cause. Removed Rat Park and its unsupported implied generalization. |
| Wilson disease is proved by one copper ring and cured by chelation | Medical example | [NIDDK causes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/wilson-disease/symptoms-causes), [diagnosis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/wilson-disease/diagnosis) and [treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/wilson-disease/treatment) describe ATP7B, multiple tests and continuing treatment. A ring may be absent, ceruloplasmin may be normal, and injury may not reverse. Corrected the caption and figure as well as the prose. |
| Kappa 0.28 means coin-toss accuracy | Statistical measurement | Read [Regier's full original report](https://psychologyrocks.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/regier2013.pdf), especially tables 2 and 4, and [Clarke's methods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23111546/) in its full author PDF. Kappa measures agreement beyond that expected by chance. Added sample labels and reported intervals; retained the investigators' questionable interpretation without converting it to percent correct. Proposed DSM-5 criteria and separate interviews limit generalization. |
| Homosexuality was removed by an arbitrary 1973 membership vote | Historical finding | Read [Drescher's complete historical account](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4695779/) through Europe PMC's full-text XML. Distinguished the 1973 board decision, 1974 referendum and 1987 removal of the later category. Research, activism and a reconsideration of impairment contributed. The judgment about prejudice is identified by its historical context rather than used to discredit every category. |
| Bereavement and RDoC overturn ordinary diagnosis | Classification and research-program facts | [APA's bereavement document](https://www.psychiatry.org/File%20Library/Psychiatrists/Practice/DSM/APA_DSM-5-Depression-Bereavement-Exclusion.pdf) requires distinguishing grief from depression. [NIMH's RDoC description](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc) explicitly concerns research. Removed the unsupported general verdict and the Rosenhan allegation tangent. |
| Pellagra disappeared from psychiatry when diet was proved | Historical example | [NIH history](https://irp.nih.gov/catalyst/27/6/from-the-annals-of-nih-history) supports Goldberger's work beginning in 1914 and niacin's identification in 1937. Kept the image and corrected its caption; removed the specialty-departure story. |
| Diagnostic categories have uncertain boundaries but can guide care | Editorial interpretation | Kept this as an argument about usefulness, course and revision. It does not follow automatically from missing biomarkers or one reliability coefficient. No personal experience, expertise or clinical observations were invented. |

## Residual limits

The full proprietary DSM-5-TR was not available as a single source. Classification was checked against APA's public documents and current guidance; sketches were checked against clinical references and explicitly remain incomplete. The 2013 field trials do not establish present-day accuracy across all settings. No underlying datasets were reanalyzed.

The eating-disorder article's full methods and limitations were accessible. Wiley exposed Degenhardt's methods abstract, results, references and supporting-information descriptions, but not the full article through the available route. The post therefore makes no causal treatment claim or exact cross-diagnosis ranking from it. The 1943 penicillin record establishes publication chronology; no ward-population outcome is inferred from that record.

The genetic and rare medical examples cannot tell an individual reader which tests to seek. The post ends on what evidence predicts and when a diagnosis might need revision, not on a promise of diagnostic certainty.

## Proposed archive-card copy

What a psychiatric diagnosis can tell you, where biological tests help, and why clinicians sometimes disagree. A sourced guide to common conditions, diagnostic boundaries and useful questions.
