The six retained tools have narrower, visible scope:

- Triage distinguishes emergency services, immediate assessment and contacting the clinician. A well-appearing young febrile infant is not automatically assigned an ambulance, but still needs immediate assessment. Breathing effort or poor responsiveness overrides numerical reassurance.
- Medicine lookup requires actual age, clinician confirmation, weight and exact supported concentration. It blocks invalid values and unsupported age/strength combinations rather than substituting a dose. It is a chart lookup, not a prescription.
- ORS requires the clinician's Plan B and reassessment arrangement, suitable age and weight. It does not infer dehydration severity. The checked arithmetic is 600 mL over four hours for 8 kg under that plan.
- Growth compares selected measurements with monthly WHO coefficients, interpolates between months and blocks invalid or pre-due-date corrected ages. It recovers after invalid input rather than destroying its output children. Thirty incorrect monthly rows were repaired; numerical matching at those rows is not a promise of clinical precision.
- Vaccine planning uses calendar months, clamps month ends and leap dates, rejects future birth dates, and treats a passed date as something to check in the record. Country grids omit catch-up rules and many risk-specific details.
- Well-baby comparison retains twelve named programmes and twenty-one direct official table links. A dedicated follow-up read every retained material programme claim against opened official guidance, reconciled matching prose and the no-JavaScript fallback, and separated regional services from national frameworks.
