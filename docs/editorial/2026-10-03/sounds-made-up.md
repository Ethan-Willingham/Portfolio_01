# The Physics That Sounds Made Up: editorial review

Completed October 4, 2026. Scope: the complete archived article, all seven SVG diagrams, thirteen photographs, captions, accessibility descriptions, metadata, inline animation and source notes.

## Reader payoff and editorial judgment

The reader gets eight explanations of relativity and quantum mechanics, tied to experiments and accompanied by drawings whose limitations are stated where they appear. The useful distinction is between a measured result, a calculation within a model and an interpretation of that calculation.

The old article had a useful selection of concepts and images, but repeated its explanations and attached much stronger claims to the experiments than those experiments establish. Its biggest problems were conceptual: a universal prohibition on reaching light speed, time dilation without a reference frame, supported bodies described as freely falling, gravity explained as pressure toward slower time, decoherence treated as outcome selection, and Bell correlations presented as proof that properties come into existence when observed. These needed a whole-post rewrite, not a few caveats in the bibliography.

The revised main path is substantially shorter. Its retained length is justified by eight different concepts and distinct evidence: satellite timing, optical clocks, interferometers, imaging, gravitational waves and a Bell experiment. The remaining source fold contains actual methods and calculation notes rather than a long list of general links.

## Actual source-access limits

- **Hawking:** Caltech's author record exposed bibliographic information and an abstract. Publisher and Project Euclid routes did not yield a usable full paper in this session. A separate mirror provided the complete 22-page 1975 paper; its assumptions and derivation sections were read. The post labels that linked copy as a mirror.
- **Fein:** The Nature landing page provided an abstract with access or rendering limits. The Leiden-hosted accepted manuscript provided the full six-page paper and methods. The article links that usable manuscript.
- **Werner (1989):** The APS page exposed its abstract and required authorization for the full article. It was not treated as a full-text source; Horodecki's accessible full paper supports the optional mixed-state definition instead.
- **Bell (1964):** CERN's scanned-paper endpoint returned an Anubis anti-bot page. It was not bypassed, and its full text was not treated as read. The retained explanation uses the opened SEP derivation and Hensen's complete original experiment instead.
- **Frisch and Smith (1963):** The MIT scan and another institutional mirror failed to provide usable full text in this session. Abstract-level search material was not treated as methods verification. No numerical result from that experiment remains; the muon passage uses the opened PDG compilation and Feynman's derivation.
- The attempted NIST 2022 GPS URL and an Einstein Online geometry URL failed; the opened Ashby review, university GPS explanation, original NIST clock paper and Einstein Online equivalence-principle page replace them. An Einstein 1905 translation timed out and the Nobel page failed; the corresponding historical runways and prize tangent were removed rather than supported by snippets. An older NASA Webb page mostly exposed navigation, so the complete NASA Science NIRCam page was used.

No retained central number depends on an inaccessible source. Historical image credits were retained, not re-licensed or represented as a newly completed rights audit. Eclipse plate errors were not independently recomputed; telescope reconstructions and lattice calculations were not rerun. Their published methods and limits, rather than a new analysis claim, support the text.
