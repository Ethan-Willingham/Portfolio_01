## Result and scope

The reader can find an urgent warning sign, the next practical care step and the relevant official source without reading a textbook. The main route stays short. Each of the eighteen substantive care chapters has one optional reference containing concise coverage of its existing topics. All twenty-two chapters, 177 subsection headings, 419 anchor IDs, twenty-three figure hooks, six tool hooks and twenty-nine image instances remain. Images, alternative text, credits, archive URL, banner and shared presentation contract were preserved.

I read the original reference sequentially through all twenty-two chapters, including every optional subsection, nested disclosure, caption, fallback, glossary and source passage. I then read the complete retained reference in chapter order and separately compared every final generated figure's tables, source identities, notes, accessible description and SVG labels with its static caption and fallback. No chapter is treated as complete from a representative sample. The complete subsection inventory below records the actual retained headings and their direct source links.

The original optional reference repeated advice already given nearby, supplied numerical certainty from sources that did not establish it, turned observational findings into instructions, and used staged disputes, research-process claims and confidence badges as substitutes for limits. Some captions and generated figures repeated errors that had been corrected in the main route. A details panel or a final disclaimer did not make those instructions accurate.

The new reference retains useful distinctions: breastfeeding intake versus a feeding-rate survey, safe sleep versus descriptive sleep distributions, screening versus diagnosis, trial populations versus general home care, chronological versus corrected age, and programme descriptions versus legal or financial entitlement. Where the source did not support a numerical row, that row was removed rather than kept with an unrelated citation.

## Source-access record and limits

I used current official guidance for retained medical, legal, policy and benefit instructions. For numerical trial/review effects, I opened the actual original methods and results rather than treating an abstract or a saved audit excerpt as fresh evidence. I checked article title, authors, DOI/PMID where available, study population and the named table before accepting a fetch. Fetch success alone did not count as access.

Full original papers or full original mirrors read for the relevant methods/results were Flaherman/NEWT, LEAP, LEAP-Trio, Iglowstein, Hirshkowitz/NSF, Blair, Carpenter, Park, Vermillet, Hill, Sung, WHO motor-development, Shattock, Paulson and Bazemore, the two SUID papers, van Schaik and CA-CODE. Large official reports/datasets were read for the particular methods, definitions and rows retained, not automatically cover to cover. The figure ledger states those scopes. Numerical values were compared with the tables; I did not reproduce the studies' statistical analyses or infer formal clinical approval.

The van Schaik cultural comparison used the correct [2020 original](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cad.20336), also obtained as full PMC7318283 XML. Methods concern selected middle-class samples of thirty-three Dutch and forty-one US mothers, data collected in 2001 to 2005, diaries/interviews and different two-/six-month groups. This is not a paired experiment establishing a national routine's sleep effect. The claimed extra two hours was prior research discussed by the paper, not established by those interviews, and was removed.

Two retained sources have explicit abstract-only scope: Gradisar's trial and the Cochrane 2018 diet review. Gradisar supplies qualitative outcomes only, with its age/sample and unavailable full results disclosed. Cochrane supplies qualitative uncertainty only and is labeled as an abstract. No unexamined quantitative row is hidden behind either citation. Publisher restrictions, browser verification pages and failed downloads were not treated as methods access. Original mirrors were used where their title/DOI/content established identity; the Hill/Sung copies and source host limits are noted above. Supplemental appendices, participant datasets, code and most secondary references were not independently read or rerun.

The focused country report and root's prior chart/tool ledger are explicit dependencies, not material quietly claimed as newly verified here. In particular, this follow-up did not reopen every programme source, every historical annual vaccine bulletin or every WHO tool coefficient. The corresponding corrected values and qualifications were preserved and compared with the final output. Sources retained merely as navigation or broader official resources are identified by their actual purpose; their presence is not a claim that every linked page or reference in an original bibliography was checked. Broad unused prestige bibliography entries were removed.
