## Result

The reader can choose one of twelve named programmes and see its contacts, delivery, records and direct official guidance. The selected detail appears beside the selector, so choosing a programme does not scroll the reader away from the control. The table still highlights the selected row. Twenty-one substantive source links are present in the table, with the selected programme's links repeated in its detail. A no-JavaScript table preserves all twelve programmes and the same twenty-one source links.

The comparison no longer claims that its rows are comparable national visit totals. It identifies England, Ontario, Victoria, Helsinki, Gooi en Vechtstreek and Shinjuku City where the actual source concerns that jurisdiction. Sweden and Finland have separate rows. The label, caption and accessible name derive the programme count from the rows rather than claiming eleven countries while combining two of them.

## Short adjacent audit

The special-circumstances main answer is retained unchanged. It correctly directs readers to an individual written care plan, names corrected-age/tool limits and avoids promising jurisdiction-independent benefits, parentage or immigration outcomes. This is a review of that main answer, not a new verification of every specialist paragraph hidden beneath it.

Three consequential adjacent screening claims were corrected from the opened AAP periodicity schedule:

- A developmental screening tool supplements surveillance. It does not replace it at nine, eighteen or thirty months. Concerns between routine ages also warrant screening.
- Blood-pressure measurement before age three is appropriate for specified risk conditions. Removed the blanket implication that infant checks never include it.
- Removed the claim that a twelve-month hemoglobin is the only routine blood test in the first eighteen months, which omitted newborn bloodspot screening. The paragraph now distinguishes newborn screening, anemia screening and risk/requirement-based lead tests.

The unsourced current-looking 35-percent developmental-screening statistic was removed rather than retained as a national delivery rate.

The optional Dutch-US routine paragraph next to the world-systems passage also overclaimed causality. The complete [van Schaik 2020 article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cad.20336) was opened, including methods and the interpretation of diaries/interviews. The replacement describes 33 Dutch and 41 US mothers from selected middle-class communities, data collected in 2001 to 2005, and an observational association. Removed the claim that a national cultural style causes an extra two hours of sleep and the unsupported rule that it only works inside an entire national policy setting. The earlier two-hour finding was discussed as prior research in that paper, not established by its own comparative interviews.

## Limits and length

This selection is a practical route to official programme guidance. It does not measure national uptake, legal entitlement, costs, appointment availability, clinical quality or outcomes. Exact appointments and any extra care still need the family's local team. Ontario intentionally supplies a narrower programme entry point rather than a fabricated complete calendar. A nurse-and-doctor age window may correspond to more than one encounter. The country fallback is available without JavaScript and duplicates the live table's source-backed facts; it is hidden during ordinary scripted reading.

The matching static prose was shortened substantially by removing universal comparisons, the duplicate six-country table and the history/funding/uptake list. The no-JavaScript fallback contains more rows because it now mirrors all twelve actual choices. All of these passages remain inside the existing optional reference chapters, so the default main reading route is unchanged. The inherited card description and main-route estimate remain appropriate; no archive-card or shared metadata edit was needed.
