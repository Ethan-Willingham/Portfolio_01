# Weather editorial revision

## Payoff and final title

**How far can we forecast the weather?**

The reader learns why predicting a storm's exact path, estimating a wetter week, and projecting a changing climate have different useful horizons. The tour explains the physical processes, the observing system, error growth, and the practical value of ensemble forecasts. The optional Lorenz model illustrates sensitivity without pretending to measure an atmospheric deadline.

Proposed archive card, 25 words:

> An illustrated tour of the physics, observations, and uncertainty behind weather forecasts, with ensembles, changing forecast horizons, and a live Lorenz model you can adjust.

## Original problems and revision

The original title said weather could never be predicted, while the body celebrated operational forecasts. It repeatedly presented two weeks as a mathematical ceiling for every forecast, gave moisture its own one-week ceiling, and misreported a 2019 experiment's improvement as one or two days. Large molecule and turbulence counts appeared to establish those limits without a derivation. Other errors included universal statements about cloud droplets, eruption prediction, storm warnings, and every known planetary atmosphere.

The numerical ceiling also shaped the examples incorrectly. Balloon stations stood in for the entire observing system. Ensemble members were described as equally believable futures whose agreement guaranteed confidence. The hurricane cone was equated with ensemble spread. AI results were described as broad superiority without a variable, test, or dated operational example. The distant-future ending added another unsupported deadline and a second rhetorical conclusion.

I rewrote every generated card and appendix entry. The 21 numbered illustrations remain, with the useful mechanisms retained: pressure gradients and rotation, ocean heat storage, latent heat, cloud radiation, turbulent mixing, aerosols, terrain, surface memory, assimilation, and numerical calculation. Statements about forecast skill now identify the quantity and scope. A dated satellite experiment replaces the unsupported budget and staffing claims. The final turn explains averages and conditional climate projections.

The source helper existed but was never called. Every card now renders its supporting links. The hero uses the shared editorial stylesheet, with the title, upright standfirst, and reading metadata in order. The circular Earth image follows the hero. The main prose is Century Supra at 1.27rem and 1.72 line height; the model retains its dedicated interface fonts.

The dormant DIA data and rendering helper were unreachable. I read their captions and labels, then removed the duplicate explanations and their unused presentation rules. Their asset files remain untouched. The active circulation schematic, orbital illustration, image captions, and enlarged-image credits remain. The opening Earth credit is corrected from DSCOVR EPIC to the 2002 composite; the WASP-76b picture is explicitly an artist's impression. The Lorenz contributor credit is retained beside the figure.

## Limits

This is an explanatory post, not an evaluation of a live local weather forecast. The forecast-horizon papers use particular models, periods, fields, and scores. The Lorenz animation is a numerical illustration; its exact long-time path depends on integration and floating-point error, and it gives no forecast-day conversion. Existing image assets and credits were preserved, but I did not independently rebuild the full license and provenance record for every photograph. The post still requires JavaScript for the generated tour, as before. Shared index and archive-card copy were not edited.
