# Philosophy collection editorial review

This report records the initial collection revision. The later [Socrates and Plato context revision](socrates-context-revision-2026-10-04.md) expands and restructures that article for readers with no prior philosophy background; its current wording and length differ from the table below.

Fourteen posts received separate editorial assignments using GPT-6.1 Sol with extra-high reasoning, followed by an independent second review. Each assignment checked the original passages, substantive claims and interpretations, then rewrote and separately edited the prose.

The first passes recorded 452 source checks. The counted text fell from 63,597 to 31,820 words, a 50% reduction. These are editorial and passage counts, not a claim that every philosophical dispute is settled. The full evidence and remaining limits are recorded in [the source audit](philosophy-source-audit-2026-10-04.json).

The presentation follows [The Hebrew Bible](https://ethanwillingham.com/hebrew-bible.html) and [The Tao Te Ching](https://ethanwillingham.com/tao-te-ching.html): centered large serif titles, a spacious opening, quiet metadata and narrow reading columns. Shared palette and self-hosted typefaces are preserved.

## Corrections by post

### Plato's cave and the death of Socrates

- Rebuilt all eight stops around particular passages rather than a repeated examination/cost/cure moral. Replaced the movie-theater opening with the less familiar requirement that the ideal city's educated philosophers return to govern.
- Separated Plato's literary Socrates from the historical person. Identified the Apology as Plato's version of the defense speech, placed Phaedo with the usual middle-period grouping, and qualified the chronology as a scholarly model.
- Replaced the absolute claim that Socrates only asked questions with his actual admission of limited ignorance. Kept craftsmen's genuine expertise and his positive moral judgment at Apology 29b.
- Removed exact jury counts, confidence that exile or a modest fine would have saved him, and the implication that the Prytaneum proposal was his only alternative. Explained the later guaranteed fine at 38b.

Limits: No surviving accusers' speeches establish their motives or prove a concealed political prosecution. Plato's speeches and prison scene cannot be independently verified as transcripts of the historical Socrates.

### Aristotle and the good life

- Replaced the promotion-and-money opening with the Ethics own qualification that loss of a child or friend can damage happiness. Kept external resources and luck inside the account of the good life.
- Corrected the claim that happiness is the only good desired for itself. Book 1.7 also treats pleasure, honor, reason, and virtue as goods chosen for themselves, as well as for happiness.
- Removed the argument that pleasure fails simply because it fades. Acknowledged the pleasures internal to excellent activity and Aristotle's own contemptuous treatment of a life devoted to bodily enjoyment.
- Corrected the claim that happiness is only a verdict after death. Book 1.10 explicitly permits describing living people as happy, with qualifications about future changes.

Limits: The precise composition date and editorial history of the Ethics remain uncertain; the revised page does not date a single authoring event. The relation between the Book 1 human good and the Book 10 contemplative ideal remains disputed. The guide reports the stronger ranking without claiming a settled reconciliation.

### Euclid's Elements, step by step

- Rebuilt the seven stops around a concrete equilateral construction, its existence gap, a readable I.47 area argument, the fifth postulate, and the limits of deduction.
- Removed the claim that Euclid invented proof and the claim that all thirteen books follow from the displayed half-page without further definitions or assumptions.
- Separated definitions, construction postulates, and common notions. Labeled the common notions as paraphrases, retained Heath wording for postulates 1 to 4, and quoted postulate 5 in full with its printed-page location.
- Explained the unstated circle-intersection assumption in I.1 and superposition in I.4 without declaring the equality arguments worthless.

Limits: Euclid's biography and precise date are poorly documented; the text says usually placed around 300 BC rather than asserting invented details. The spherical SVG is explicitly a schematic projection; screen angles are not the actual angles on the sphere.

### Marcus Aurelius's Meditations

- Rewrote the introduction around 6.30 and all 28 passage explanations around the actual arguments, including providence, judgment, obligations to others, and the limits of an emperor's perspective.
- Replaced certainty about a private unpublished notebook and all entries being written on campaign with scholarly consensus and identified evidence limits.
- Reduced 137 rows from eight translators to 97 rows from five: Casaubon 28, Long 28, Haines 28, Farquharson 12, Hays 1. Removed 40 rows whose comparison value or edition verification did not justify retaining them, including the two mislocated 9.30 comparisons. Removal does not imply that all the discarded modern wording was fabricated.
- Added source links and locators to every translation; identified abridged quotes and Greek excerpts. Corrected several obvious transcription slips while disclosing punctuation normalization.

Limits: The received Greek and its online transcriptions have edition variants and occasional transcription defects. This review compares content and sources; it does not establish a new critical text or collate manuscripts. Marcus's exact composition dates, ordering, intended eventual audience, and fidelity in governing to his own principles cannot be established from the passages.

### Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau

- Replaced all island/plane-crash framing, opening quote fan and five repeated split panels with concrete book arguments.
- Corrected Hobbes on assembly sovereignty, retained self-defense, protection-dependent allegiance, and conquest-related consent; retained the weakness of checks on sovereign power.
- Corrected Locke's two surrendered powers and broad use of property; added majority/tacit consent, law and tax limits, resistance threshold, spoilage/enough-and-as-good limits, and money-based inequality.
- Separated Rousseau's conjectural history from Social Contract; distinguished sovereignty from executive government, general will from majority preference, changed forms of liberty, unanimous founding consent and excluded dissenters.

Limits: Locke's colonial land theory and precise responsibility for proslavery Carolina clauses remain scholarly disputes; the post names them rather than resolving them by assertion. The general will has competing scholarly interpretations. This short comparison follows the book's common-interest/general-law conditions and explicitly rejects any simple equation with a majority preference.

### Kant and the Categorical Imperative

- Rebuilt all seven explanations around the actual Groundwork argument, retaining IDs, navigation, quoted passages, portrait assets and dedicated-script contract.
- Distinguished good will from favorable outcomes and mere wishing; explained conforming to duty versus acting from duty without dismissing sympathy.
- Added an explicitly reconstructed maxim, contradiction in conception versus will, perfect versus imperfect duties, and the four original examples with their contested premise.
- Explained humanity as rational end-setting, merely-as-means, deception, positive duties and the limits of consent; corrected Preis/value and removed unsupported human-rights genealogy.

Limits: The mixed-motive reading, equivalence of formulations, rational-capacity basis of dignity, and success/continuity of Kant's freedom arguments are contested and remain marked as such. The lying objection is an editorial argument under expressly stated assumptions. Timmermann's legal-context reading is attributed; it does not erase Kant's uncompromising conclusion in the constrained case.

### Marx and Engels: The Communist Manifesto

- Named Engels as coauthor in the identical title, og:title, and h1 and throughout Manifesto/German Ideology discussion.
- Separated the 1848 polemical program from the 1844 manuscripts, German Ideology, and Capital; corrected Volume I publication and posthumous editing of later volumes.
- Marked omissions and restored exact retained quote wording; supplied translator/edition and section/chapter/manuscript locators.
- Explained alienation as control over productive activity, surplus value through labor-power and a six/twelve-hour illustrative model, socially necessary time, transferred input value, and Marx's explicit nature/wealth qualification.

Limits: The precise revision history of the MIA German Ideology 1968 transcription was not checked against a scan of that edition. W. Lough's chapter translation credit is verified by the primary publisher preface reproduced at libcom; excerpt wording is verified against the linked transcription. Demographic deaths are estimates with different methods, not one comparable cross-regime total or a proof of how much responsibility belongs to Marx personally.

### John Stuart Mill

- Preserved all seven station IDs, all nine featured excerpts, collapsible difficult-case and legal-comparison panels, portrait, and navigation.
- Moved children and imperial exclusions beside the initial principle; made utility, informed consent, specific duties, definite risks, and costs of intervention explicit.
- Corrected excerpt punctuation and omissions, restored written incitement in corn-dealer example, and named Coulthard plus Mill adaptation in Humboldt epigraph.
- Added voluntary enslavement exception and public-decency qualification; removed false categorical claims in original prose.

Limits: Mill reconciliation of utility, protected freedoms, and paternalistic exceptions remains contested; the prose avoids resolving it by fiat. The precise contribution of Harriet Taylor Mill cannot be assigned from the dedication.

### Darwin's Origin of Species

- Preserved all eight stop IDs, the existing diagram and portrait, and the dedicated script contracts. No JavaScript, assets, or shared styles changed by this editor.
- Put inherited variation and reproductive success at the center; separated selection from intention, physical strength, inevitable progress and a universal origin-of-life explanation.
- Distinguished selection from descent; restored species/variety uncertainty, sterility limits, classification and structural homology. Diagram described as a hypothetical genus model.
- Kept Darwin's eye objection beside his immediate answer, verified both against original scans, and separated useful possible gradations from actual reconstructed ancestry. Added a narrowly stated modern optical model.

Limits: The page remains a selected eight-stop guide rather than an exhaustive account of all first-edition arguments or contemporary evolutionary biology. The Nilsson-Pelger model is a conditional route for optical improvement, not proof of the actual ancestry of a named eye or the first appearance of photosensitive cells.

### Nietzsche and the Case Against Morality

- Rebuilt the introduction around the specific Tertullian passage Nietzsche discusses in Genealogy I.15. Removed invented reader conversations, the atheist lunch scene, repeated series-wide claims about humility, aphoristic endings, and recurring takeaways.
- Distinguished Beyond Good and Evil (1886) from On the Genealogy of Morals (1887), and identified the earlier Gay Science and Zarathustra as the sources of the death of God, recurrence, and overman passages.
- Explained master and slave valuation as patterns that can coexist in a society and a person, with self-command and generosity as well as contempt in master valuation. Removed the simple strong-person versus weak-person partition.
- Explained ressentiment as persistent resentment that reorganizes judgment, distinguished Nietzsche's lambs-and-predators argument from a justification of human predation, and retained the objection that origin alone does not establish a rule's present value.

Limits: The replacement of Christian values by a coherent positive account is philosophically contested. The article identifies the authority, suffering, and affirmation problems instead of pretending they are settled. Will to power and recurrence have contested interpretive roles. Their conjectural or conditional formulations are distinguished from established scientific claims.

### The Myth of Sisyphus

- Explains the absurd as a relation between human demand and the world’s silence; distinguishes the absence of an accessible ultimate explanation from a proof that nothing has value.
- Separates the philosophical question about suicide from claims about why people die by suicide; removes flippant formulas and judgments of courage or worth.
- Checks February 1941 completion and October 1942 publication; removes retrospective camp knowledge from the composition context.
- Retains three distinct gallery responses with verified O’Brien extracts, explicit translation labels, section locators, and paraphrase clearly separated from quotation.

Limits: The English source is a searchable transcription with scanning errors and pagination different from printed editions. Whitespace and a scanning hyphen were normalized during exact quotation checks; no published print edition was independently collated. The French source is an augmented later edition containing the Kafka appendix, so it is used for the central distinctions and is explicitly not presented as the untouched 1942 edition.

### Can You Argue Your Way to God?

- Rebuilt the article around the difference between practical reasons to seek faith and evidential reasons to believe Christianity.
- Corrected Pensées publication to the posthumous 1670 edition, kept composition date uncertain, and identified Brunschvicg 233, Lafuma 418, Sellier 680 and the W.F. Trotter translation.
- Reconstructed the wager with infinite salvation and finite other outcomes. The matrix explicitly omits rival religions and does not assert that Pascal’s argument requires infinite punishment.
- Explained why the final expected-utility argument requires positive probability rather than 50/50 odds; retained substantial finite costs, voluntary-belief difficulties, Pascal’s recommended practices, and the ambiguity of vous abêtira.

Limits: Pascal’s fragment composition date is not securely fixed; the page explicitly distinguishes it from his death and the posthumous 1670 publication. The mathematical table is a stated modern simplification with finite utilities outside salvation, not a facsimile of Pascal’s notes or a proof that Christianity is true.

### David Deutsch

- Opened with opposite seasons and the physical role of axial tilt, then defined varying the explanatory content. Removed the excuse analogy and claim that this one test is the whole book.
- Distinguished testability, being hard to vary, and success in empirical tests. The Persephone account itself makes testable predictions; mere falsifiability cannot distinguish it from the tilt account.
- Removed the claim that every good explanation has universal reach. Retained the specific-scope counterexample of geography and the independent claim of universal laws.
- Presented human explanatory universality as Deutsch's philosophical claim, not a theorem about brains following from computer universality. Corrected numeral universality to arbitrarily large integers. Removed the unsupported implication that the genetic code's universality is settled.

Limits: The publicly accessible ebook PDF and chapter transcriptions are unofficial hosts, not a publisher-hosted full edition. The PDF identifies the selected 2011 edition and matching ISBN; two glossary quotations were independently checked on the author's official site. Human explanatory universality, exceptional cosmic significance, optimism, and the sweeping static/dynamic historical interpretation remain philosophical arguments in the book, not empirical consensus or proved entailments.

### Sapiens, Read Skeptically

- Opened on the concrete Peugeot legal-entity example, then explained shared rules, institutions and enforcement. Removed the worthless-paper illustration, commercial reach paragraph and announced skeptical stance.
- Retained and sourced Hebrew 2011 / English 2014 publication dates; corrected cognitive dating to Harari's 70,000 to 30,000-year window and his uncertainty about its cause.
- Placed the Dunbar dispute and region/species differences in extinction evidence beside the respective claims. The Lion-man demonstrates imaginative representation, not a proven first religion.
- Rewrote all five gallery cards and removed the JavaScript quote/lesson layer. Cards explicitly distinguish paraphrase, explanation and philosophical commentary while keeping chips, arrows, keyboard behavior and the no-JS list.

Limits: There was no Sapiens topic dossier under research, only research/prompts/sapiens.json; the full brief was read and independent primary/official checks were made. Archaeological evidence cannot identify the first story or establish the earliest religious cooperation. The post explicitly does not date them.

## Independent review and validation

All fourteen posts passed desktop (1440px) and phone (375px) checks for title metadata, centered headings, text gutters, duplicate IDs, local assets and horizontal overflow. Navigation, source disclosures, passage selection and map/gallery controls were exercised. Marcus was checked across all 28 selections and 97 translation rows; the social-contract comparison across all five questions and fifteen citations. Deep-link loads and reduced-motion navigation were checked. Euclid's figures remain visible without JavaScript.

The hub was checked for all fourteen links, scoped search and keyboard selection of Marcus and social-contract deep links. These tests served the exact scoped publication tree, including committed shared assets.
