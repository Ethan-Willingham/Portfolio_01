# How Much Is Enough?

`enough.html` is an explorable post in In Progress. It has a whole public-domain painting, an opening topic selector and 12 cards: walking, exercise, protein, sets, sleep, fruit and vegetables, fiber, income, savings, work, alcohol and smoking. The owner removed the personal form, separate slider controls and Mind section. Shared fonts, links, navigation and footer follow `what-you-get-used-to.html`. The chooser is `enough-lab.html`.

The current scientific decisions and primary sources are documented in [ENOUGH_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md](ENOUGH_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md). That report is downloadable from the post. Research artifacts and historical reviews live in the main checkout’s ignored `research/enough/v2/` directory. Historical AI reader reviews do not certify the current edits.

## Current presentation

Protein uses the same graph component as the other topics: a dashed curve, published pointwise confidence shading, labeled axes and a movable marker. Its 111 approximate readings come from Tagawa 2021 Figure 2(h), the fully adjusted resistance-training panel. Intake is total g/lb/day; outcome is absolute modeled lean-mass change in lb across trial arms. It is not the causal effect of adding protein, an individual prediction or a hypertrophy percentage. The source adjusts for age, sex, duration and weight change, including a potential mediator. Other source panels differ, and the narrow pointwise band does not include all model uncertainty. Native source image coordinates, calibration, checksum and original kg values are stored with the data. No new spline is fitted, hard plateau imposed or tail extrapolated.

The ISSN recommendation of 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg/day remains separate in the headline, about 0.65 to 0.9 g/lb/day. It does not define the curve or its shading. Nunes 2022’s 62 resistance-exercise trials support a small added-protein lean-mass effect, but cannot establish a daily cutoff. Antonio 2014 tested about 2 versus 0.82 g/lb/day for eight weeks in 30 completers and found no statistically clear extra fat-free-mass change. This small trial is cited as separate evidence; its results are not spliced into the Tagawa curve, whose training panel ends near 1.4 g/lb/day. The old Morton arm-level segmented model is retained only in `supportingEvidence.protein2018`.

Walking has one card and one matching opening preview, driven by the same Paluch 2022 age curves. Under 60 defaults to 8,000 steps; 60+ defaults to 6,000. Age and selected amount stay synchronized, and each age remembers its own marker. Both views use a step axis from zero to 16,000. Both compare estimated mortality risk with about 6,500 steps/day, the U.S. adult average measured in NHANES 2005 to 2006, rounded from 6,540. The survey year is visible beside both graphs. This is a comparison at the average step count, not the average American mortality risk or a claim about the current national average. The national survey used a filtered accelerometer count; device definitions affect averages.

The risk axis is linear, with signed percentage labels: “300% higher,” “100% higher,” “Same risk” and “50% lower.” Equal vertical spacing represents equal percentage-point differences. The nearby Same risk and 50% lower labels sit on opposite sides of their grid lines to leave readable space without distorting the scale. Every opening, card and accessible risk readout names the U.S. average step count. There is no zero-mortality tick or fixed reference marker. Only the movable selection circle and its vertical guide appear. The owner removed the fixed diamond and prominent Compare label. Narrow plots show only 0, 8,000 and 16,000 step ticks.

Raw hazard-ratio points and source confidence limits remain unchanged and keep the study’s original 5,000- and 3,000-step referenceDose fields. Displayed reduction is 100 times (1 minus HR at the selected dose divided by HR at 6,500). Recalculating contrast intervals needs covariance unavailable from the published figure, so walking has no confidence shading against the new reference. The short drawer explains this, and original limits remain downloadable. The near-zero tail remains dashed; selecting zero shows no estimate because the source strokes begin just above zero. No new spline, extrapolation or mortality evidence is added. The former #steps-longer hash reaches this card. Ding 2025 remains supporting evidence, not a second walking section.

Exercise retains Garcia’s author-deposited curve, now with confidence shading. The opening, card and accessible readouts explicitly say lower risk of dying than with no exercise; the selected percentage comes from the same original curve. Its headline gives HHS guidance of 150 to 300 moderate minutes/week. The sets model remains dashed with a 95% credible band; its tail above 25 is visibly marked as sparse. Its axis names the outcome, “Muscle size vs no training, %,” and the readout gives the modeled size difference in a sentence. Its visible caption explains the percentage with a concrete example: if a muscle stays at 100 units without training, 4.2% means about 104.2 with training. This is over the studies, not per week. The original 10-versus-zero contrast remains 4.18%; it divides post/pre size ratios, not percentages of growth. Zero on the chart means no extra growth from training, not no muscle. Sleep retains the named older self-report curve with its confidence band; the arbitrary six-hour highlight is removed and the headline uses adult guidance.

All card graphs share a 240-pixel canvas, labeled axes and a movable circle. Fruit, income, alcohol and smoking retain published group estimates as dots and 95% interval whiskers. Dashed straight joins connect those points for visual continuity; their key states that the joins guide the eye. They are not fitted dose-response curves, selection still snaps to published groups, and no continuous confidence band is invented between them. Income uses actual 100-point feeling scores and each group’s own interval; the axis is explicitly zoomed to 55 to 70. Fiber uses Yao 2023’s updated per-10-gram linear mortality summary and slope limits. The old Reynolds model remains in `supportingEvidence.fiber2019`. Work is a dashed historical model. Its axis begins at zero, while the source-supported line begins at 24 hours, the shortest week observed. The blank 0 to 24 region is labeled No data and returns no estimate when selected. Output means additional index units relative to a 24-hour week, not total output. No source coefficients or supported values change. Savings is dashed arithmetic; its shaded alternatives are assumptions, not confidence limits. The curve is a smooth approximation, while its readout rounds up to whole year-end contributions.

No live chart displays an exact 90% enough marker or treats a shown endpoint as 100% of possible benefit. Historical bounded calculations remain reproducible in the data and math module. They are not medical, financial or work recommendations. Card starting markers come from explicit `view.default` values rather than a calculated 90% crossing.

The owner removed the opening “What does enough mean?” disclosure. Each topic evidence drawer has two brief paragraphs, primary links and a source-data download. Full study counts, units, subgroup estimates, extraction methods and exclusions remain in the data and audit. Study populations and overlapping reviews must not be added into a grand evidence total.

## Interaction and checks

Clicking or dragging selects an amount directly. Arrow keys change it; Home and End select endpoints. Touch taps and horizontal drags select doses while vertical gestures scroll. Pointer exit clears pointer-origin decoration only, preserving the value, focus and captured dragging; keyboard focus remains visible. There is no storage or submission.

Run `node tools/test-enough-data.mjs` and, with Playwright on NODE_PATH, `node tools/test-enough-browser.cjs`. The harness owns an ephemeral server and `/Users/ethan/.local/bin/agent-chrome-for-testing`, closing both in `finally`. It checks 320x812, 375x812, 768x900 and 1440x900, including source values, protein figure conversion, source intervals, re-referenced walking math and absent fabricated contrast bands, self-contained walking readouts and absence of fixed reference markers, unsupported work-hour gaps, group-only joins, chart controls, touch scrolling, pointer-exit behavior, shared reference styles, short disclosures, no removed panels and no missing resources. `ENOUGH_BASE_URL` runs the same checks against the deployed site. Automation does not certify real screen readers or physical devices.

Painting alternatives are Bruegel’s The Harvesters, Homer’s Breezing Up and Chardin’s Fruit, Jug, and a Glass. All are shown whole. Defaults are Bruegel, walking first and life-area colors.
