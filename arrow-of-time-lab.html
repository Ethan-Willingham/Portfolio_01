Draw a shape, choose its colors, then render its dissolve and return.

Use your mouse or touch. Keyboard: arrows move, Enter toggles the pen.

Scheduled: 4,320 forward steps, then 4,320 inverse steps. At the default pace, each direction takes three active minutes. A completed return is followed by an explicit reset to the next authored image. Your drawing repeats until you choose another image. Timeline jumps use stored exact states; normal playback computes every update.

Energy couples both time layers. Entropy is an 8 × 8 block estimator. Zoom reveals the individual cells; the display has no trails. No equilibrium criticality or fractal dimension is claimed.

The moth comes back, down to its last spin. Two layers of a rectangular lattice evolve by one reversible rule: when four neighbors split two and two, a spin in the older layer flips. The image dissolves into moving domains. Drag the timeline to any part of its six-minute dissolve and return, or press Play to watch. The sequence is prepared from the exact rule, and Instruments checks both layers at the end. Draw your own replaces the starting image.

Cyan, violet, pink and amber show the mean of both time layers. All four inks and the navy background are adjustable. The close view resolves individual cells; zoom out for the whole image. Brief transitions between computed states soften the motion. The microscopic information survives even when the coarse image disappears; this authored orbit is not a thermal sample.
