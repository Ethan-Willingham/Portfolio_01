The Old Masters

old master (n.)
1.a European painter working before about 1800. 2.a painting by one. This gallery also includes later painters.
Plate I · 1597

The Musicians

Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
92.1 × 118.4 cm (about 36 × 47 in.)
Held at
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York. Rogers Fund, 1952.

Caravaggio killed a man in a fight in Rome in 1606 and fled the city. He spent the rest of his life painting in Naples, Malta, and Sicily while seeking a papal pardon. He died in 1610 before he could return to Rome.

Plate II · probably after 1827

Landscape with a Plowed Field and a Village

Georges Michel

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on paper, mounted on canvas
Size
51.1 × 70.2 cm (about 20 × 28 in.)
Held at
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Michel worked as a restorer at the Louvre, where he studied Dutch landscapes by Ruisdael, Van Goyen, and Rembrandt. This village is invented. He painted on paper mounted on canvas, a cheaper way to get the smooth surface of the wood panels those painters used.

Plate III · 1871

Breton Brother and Sister

William-Adolphe Bouguereau

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
129.2 × 89.2 cm (about 51 × 35 in.)
Held at
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York. Catharine Lorillard Wolfe Bequest, 1887.

Bouguereau based this painting on sketches he made in Brittany in the late 1860s, then completed it in his studio. The children are posed in traditional Breton clothing. American collectors bought many of these rural scenes; this one came to the Met through Catharine Lorillard Wolfe's bequest.

Plate IV · 1899

Girl Holding Lemons

William-Adolphe Bouguereau

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
65.9 × 49.8 cm (about 26 × 20 in.)
Held at
The Israel Museum, Jerusalem. Gift in memory of Goldie Weisbord.

In the year he painted this, Bouguereau took his son Paul to Menton on the French Riviera. Paul had tuberculosis, and his doctors recommended sea air. During the stay, Bouguereau found a room in their hotel where he could paint.

Plate V · 1888

By the Stream (Au Bord du Ruisseau)

William-Adolphe Bouguereau

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Painted
1888
Provenance
Bought from the artist by Joseph Barrigan of Providence on May 30, 1888. M.S. Rau catalog.

The girl's red stockings lie beside her on the bank. Bouguereau painted the skin, cloth, and plants with almost no visible brushwork. His reputation declined after his death, as tastes shifted toward modern painting. Exhibitions in the late twentieth century brought renewed interest in his work.

Plate VI · 1865

The Veteran in a New Field

Winslow Homer

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
61.3 × 96.8 cm (about 24 × 38 in.)
Held at
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York. Adelaide Milton de Groot Bequest, 1967.
Painted
Summer 1865, after Lee's surrender at Appomattox and Lincoln's assassination.

Homer first painted a scythe with a wooden cradle, a set of spokes that caught the cut wheat. He painted the spokes out, though they now show through the aging paint. The simpler blade resembles the Grim Reaper's scythe. The man's Union Army jacket and canteen lie in the lower right corner.

Plate VII · 1772

The Shipwreck

Claude-Joseph Vernet

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
113.5 × 162.9 cm (about 45 × 64 in.)
Held at
National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. Acquired in 2000.
Commissioned
November 1771, by Henry, 8th Lord Arundell of Wardour.

Lord Arundell commissioned this with a companion painting, Moonlight, for his house in Wiltshire. The pair put a shipwreck beside a calm harbor. They stayed in the family until 1952 and were separated after that sale.

Plate VIII · 1820

Salisbury Cathedral from Lower Marsh Close

John Constable

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Held at
National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. Andrew W. Mellon Collection, 1937.
Provenance
Unsold in Constable's lifetime. Sold at his studio auction in 1838.

In 1821 and 1822, Constable painted cloud studies on Hampstead Heath, often recording the date, time, wind direction, and weather on the back. These were small oil paintings made outdoors. This view of Salisbury dates from just before that period.

Plate IX · 1839

The Rape of Proserpine

Joseph Mallord William Turner

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
92 × 122.2 cm (about 36 × 48 in.)
Held at
National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. Gift of Mrs. Watson B. Dickerman, 1951.
First shown
Royal Academy of Arts, London, 1839.

Turner showed this at the Royal Academy in 1839, with a passage from Ovid's Metamorphoses in the exhibition catalog. The subject is Pluto's abduction of Proserpine. The figures are small enough that you can take in the whole landscape before noticing what's happening at the lower left.

Plate X · 1834

Venice: The Dogana and San Giorgio Maggiore

Joseph Mallord William Turner

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
91.5 × 122 cm (about 36 × 48 in.)
Held at
National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. Widener Collection.
First shown
Royal Academy Summer Exhibition, 1834.
Commissioned by
Henry McConnel, a Manchester cotton manufacturer.

Henry McConnel commissioned this view of Venice, then ordered Keelmen Heaving in Coals by Moonlight to hang beside it. The second painting shows men loading coal at night in Newcastle. He sold both to John Naylor in 1849. When he tried to buy one back in 1861, Naylor refused.

Plate XI · c. 1804

Boats Carrying Out Anchors and Cables to the Dutch Men of War

Joseph Mallord William Turner

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
101.6 × 130.8 cm (about 40 × 51 in.)
Held at
National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. Corcoran Collection (William A. Clark).

Seven men work the small boat in the foreground, with warships farther out. Four are rowing. The boat sits low between the waves, and another follows behind it. Turner gives most of the canvas to the sea and sky.

Plate XII · 1754

Intérieur du Port de Marseille

Claude-Joseph Vernet, from the Ports of France series.

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
165 × 263 cm (about 5 ft 5 in. × 8 ft 8 in.)
Held at
Musée national de la Marine, Paris, on deposit from the Louvre.
Commissioned by
King Louis XV of France in 1753, for the Ports of France series.

Vernet included himself, his wife Virginie, and their son Livio among the people on the quay. Louis XV's commission required him to show the work of each port in detail. Here that means cargo in jars and bales, grain piled on the quay, and ships from around the Mediterranean.

Plate XIII · 1850

The Ninth Wave

Ivan Aivazovsky (Hovhannes Aivazian).

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Size
221 × 332 cm (about 7 ft 3 in. × 10 ft 11 in.)
Held at
The State Russian Museum, St. Petersburg. Transferred from the Hermitage in 1897.

The title comes from a sailors' belief that the ninth wave is the largest and most dangerous. The survivors cling to a broken mast as daylight reaches the water. Aivazovsky painted his seascapes from memory in his studio; he said a breaking wave moved too quickly to paint from life.

Plate XIV · c. 1852

The Hamlet of Optevoz

Charles-François Daubigny

Click to zoom
Medium
Oil on canvas
Held at
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York. Robert Graham Dun Bequest, 1900.
Setting
Optevoz, a village in southeastern France.

Daubigny based this painting on drawings he made at Optevoz in 1852. He'd first visited the village in 1849. One of the preparatory sketches is also at the Met; a later painting in Philadelphia shows the same view from farther back, in different light.
