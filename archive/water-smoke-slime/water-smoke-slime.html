Instruments

What moves the smoke?

The airflow control supplies a steady push at the left inlet. Air velocity and pressure are calculated around the pipe and current water surface. Smoke follows that flow, with no preset route or looping animation.

What this model leaves out

This is a two-dimensional approximation. Air pressure cannot push the water seal out of the bend, and gas chemistry is left out. The water toggle adds or removes water instantly, without simulating evaporation or a drain.

Click the water to dry or refill the bend. Click the inlet to switch airflow on or off. Changing the water keeps the existing smoke and air motion. Switching airflow starts a fresh comparison.

About

Three physics engines from Sluice, my mining game, share one box. Draw a wall and the water, smoke and slime all collide with it. Choose a machine from the menu at the top, or grab a slime and pull.

October 12 update: the twelve unfinished machines now have working mechanisms and comparison setups. Watch ice melt, flood the submarine, vent the suction cup, or withdraw the gripper pistons. Instruments explains each model’s approximations.

Water
Particles carry the liquid. A temporary grid exchanges their momentum and applies pressure. The MLS-MPM solver runs on WebGPU, adapted from saharan’s water demo.
Air
The water machines add a pressure solve. Room air sets zero gauge pressure, and each sealed pocket has one pressure at a fixed temperature, compressing as water takes its space. Aanjaneya, Patkar and Fedkiw’s bubble model describes this approximation.
Smoke
A WebGL fluid solver moves velocity and dye through a grid, following Pavel Dobryakov’s work. The visible smoke is dye in that flow.
Slime
A mesh of elastic connections holds its volume while it stretches and compresses, solved with XPBD on the CPU.

The instruments treat the box as 30 inches wide and 1 inch deep. This is a game simulation tuned for play: the engines trade approximate boundary motion instead of sharing one solver.

Water needs WebGPU and smoke needs WebGL; without one, the rest still runs. Space pauses, F toggles fullscreen, O shows oxygen in combustion scenes, and keys 1 to 6 choose tools.
