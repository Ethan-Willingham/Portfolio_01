A soap film’s colors tell you how thick it is. Light reflected from its two surfaces cancels at some wavelengths and adds at others. Here those colors come from the film’s thickness, the CIE color tables and daylight illumination. Drag a finger through the oval to fold the bands into swirls. Warm or cool the lower edge to change the convection. The film keeps evolving until you clear it.

What this model leaves out

This is a flat, depth-averaged fluid carrying a thickness field. It includes thermal buoyancy, viscosity, air drag and conservative thickness transport. The square simulation is mapped onto an oval with width-to-height ratio 1.618. Dragging adds momentum to the liquid. Drainage, evaporation and rupture are disabled in this persistent view. Clear supplies a fresh film.

Real soap films also have surfactant transport, surface stresses, capillarity and molecular stabilization. Those effects are absent. The 2008 experiment below heated a curved half-bubble at its equator. This model uses a different geometry. Its rising plumes are thermal convection.
