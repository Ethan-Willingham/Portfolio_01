Reference map where satellite coverage ends.

Hourly cloud images. Aurora history covers saved forecasts only.

The opening view uses your device location, with browser permission. A rounded position is remembered on this device and refreshed each visit. Coordinates stay on your device. Night clouds soften the historical city lights; their transparency is illustrative.

Hourly satellite maps: NASA GIBS (NOAA GOES and JMA Himawari) and EUMETSAT EUMETView (Meteosat). Visible and infrared brightness show white clouds over reference terrain; snow and cold ground can also appear bright. Overlapping images favour the more direct satellite view. Blank image areas fade into the reference map. Older dates use three-hour EUMETSAT maps. Contains modified EUMETSAT data, 2021 to 2026, under CC BY 4.0. Daily fallback: NASA GIBS, NOAA-21 and NOAA-20 VIIRS. City lights: NASA Black Marble, 2016.

Aurora: NOAA SWPC. The glow shows modeled probabilities above 10%, with illustrative curtain detail. A forecast does not guarantee a sighting. Clouds, daylight and local lights can hide it.

Stars: HYG 4.1, David Nash / Astronexus. 5,070 measured stars, magnitude 6 and brighter. Adapted catalog under CC BY-SA 4.0. Positions follow the selected time; point sizes and exposure are adjusted for the screen. Sun and Moon directions follow the selected time, with compressed distances. Glare illustrates a camera's response to the visible Sun. Pin clocks use local solar time.

Calculation notes.

With the globe focused, use arrow keys to rotate, plus or minus to zoom, Enter to pin the center, and Escape to exit fullscreen or clear the pin. Drag, pinch or scroll to explore. Pin times follow the Sun, rather than a civil time zone.
