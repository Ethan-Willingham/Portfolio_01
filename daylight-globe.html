With the globe focused, use arrow keys to rotate, plus or minus to zoom, Enter to pin the center, and Escape to exit fullscreen or clear the pin. Drag, pinch or scroll to explore. Pin times follow the Sun, rather than a civil time zone.

Satellites can see clouds after sunset: infrared images measure heat rather than reflected sunlight. The slider follows dated satellite images and saved aurora forecasts, with a separate timestamp for each.

Satellite imagery: EUMETSAT EUMETView, NOAA and JMA, with natural-colour RGB by day and infrared at night. Contains modified EUMETSAT data, 2021 to 2026, under CC BY 4.0. Daily fallback: NASA GIBS, NOAA-21 and NOAA-20 VIIRS. Night lights: NASA Black Marble, 2016. Aurora and Kp: NOAA SWPC. The glow illustrates modeled probabilities above 10%; it does not guarantee a sighting. Clouds, daylight and local lights can hide aurora. Sun and Moon distances are compressed; background stars are illustrative. Pin clocks use local solar time. Calculation notes.
