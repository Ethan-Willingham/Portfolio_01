Power, Story, and Love

Odysseus is trying to get home. The Prince asks how a ruler keeps power. From epic poems to advice on love, these books pay close attention to what people do when they want something.

  • Late 8th to early 7th c. BCE

    The Odyssey

    Odysseus returns to Ithaca disguised as a beggar. These readings follow his journey home and the violence that makes it possible to reclaim his household.
  • 4th c. BCE

    Plato's Symposium

    At a dinner party, Socrates reports Diotima's teaching on desire. Other guests offer different accounts of love, and Alcibiades makes the argument personal.
  • 4th to 3rd c. BCE

    The Art of War

    Sun Tzu treats war as a danger to the state that wages it. Compare fifteen passages, with Chinese alongside English translations that don't always agree.
  • Early centuries CE

    The Kama Sutra

    Vatsyayana discusses pleasure, marriage, and a courtesan's livelihood. Compare thirteen passages with Sanskrit and two English editions that sometimes give opposing advice.
  • 1513; published 1532

    The Prince

    A prince can be generous with someone else's money. Machiavelli follows the costs of ruling, including the difference between seeming virtuous and keeping people loyal.
  • 1879

    The Grand Inquisitor

    Ivan Karamazov tells a story in which the Church arrests the returning Jesus. The Inquisitor argues that people want bread and authority more than freedom.
  • 1906 and 1933-1934

    In Praise of Shadows

    Tanizaki describes a lacquer bowl in a dim room; Okakura writes about preparing tea. Read the two essays alongside the claims they make about Japanese beauty.
