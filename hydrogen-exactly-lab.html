A localized hydrogen wave packet spreads around its orbit, forms smaller packets, and partly gathers itself again. The colored contours mark increasing probability density, from cyan through violet and pink to gold. This image comes from adding 13 circular states and evolving each with its own energy. The ring comes from the highly excited circular states chosen for this packet. Their density is concentrated around an equatorial ring; the packet initially occupies an arc and later spreads around it. Hydrogen's lowest-energy 1s state has a spherical probability cloud. The shape depends on the quantum state, rather than every hydrogen atom having a doughnut shape.

What “exactly” means here, and sources

These are exact eigenstates of the fixed-nucleus, nonrelativistic Coulomb model, evaluated numerically. The finite state list, render box, f32 shader arithmetic, f16 display texture, grid interpolation, and ray samples introduce errors. Nuclear motion, relativity, and radiative corrections are omitted. The 2D section evaluates the wavefunction directly; the volume interpolates sampled positive density.

The default view draws surfaces at six fixed density levels, revealing the highest contour reached along each view ray. Directional lighting, highlights, shadows, a faint reference plane, and the cyan-to-gold palette help show the shape. These are visual aids; the plane is not part of the atom. They leave the wavefunction and its diagnostics unchanged. Soft volume and a directly evaluated 2D section are also available in Instruments. Spectral color weights the magnitudes of visible signed cross terms. A separate diagnostic marks the sign of the 2p / 3s term. Dark nodes have zero analytic density; volume interpolation can soften them.
